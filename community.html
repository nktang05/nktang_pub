Community Involvement & Interests

Camp Kesem at USC

I have been involved with USC’s Camp Kesem chapter since my freshman year. I have served as a counselor, unit leader, and am now starting my fourth year as an executive board member. As a counselor and unit leader, I developed friendships with kids whose lives are impacted by a parent's cancer through the week-long summer camp. As a board member, my responsibilities have included outreach, networking, fundraising, counselor training, meeting planning, and community engagement. Through my Kesem experience and my own journey dealing with my mom's and dad's cancers, I have learned about the importance of resilience, empathy, positivity, inclusion, and making everyone feel that despite their challenges, they are not alone.

Japanese American Advocacy

As a fifth-generation Japanese American, my family’s history is deeply shaped by the incarceration of Japanese Americans during World War II. My grandparents’ families were imprisoned at the Jerome and Rohwer camps, and my grandfather, Kanji Sahara, dedicated much of his life to ensuring these injustices are never forgotten. Growing up, I attended many events which instilled in me a strong sense of cultural identity, community responsibility, and the importance of historical remembrance. I later continued his legacy by interning with the World War II Camp Wall project, organizing over 240,000 records of incarcerated individuals to preserve their stories through data. These experiences have deeply connected me to my heritage and inspire my ongoing commitment to education, remembrance, and advocacy.

Cancer & Stem Cell Registry Advocacy

I collaborated with Asians for Miracle Marrow Matches, A3M, where I educated people in my community on the importance of joining the Bone Marrow Registry and being committed to donating bone marrow (stem cells) if matched. I made a video with interviews of stem cell transplant donors and recipients to use as an educational tool and gave presentations at several events. I focused on informing teens and young adults because they make the best donors. As ethnicity plays a major role in matching a patient to a donor, I placed emphasis on diversity as minorities and people of mixed race are underrepresented within the registry. Working with (A3M) taught me how ethnicity plays a huge role in receiving healthcare as well as how culture can influence an individual’s decision to join the registry and follow through with donating bone marrow, so it is always important to approach health topics while being educationally and culturally sensitive.

Sourdough

I got into sourdough and bread making during my free time and it quickly became one of my favorite hobbies. I love experimenting with new flavors and techniques, from different flours to creative mix-ins. I even keep a spreadsheet to track each bake so I can tweak recipes and see how they turn out. There’s something really satisfying about watching the dough rise and sharing fresh bread with friends and family.
